# Verified facts expose MOTION's stale compliance claims

As of 25 November 2026, **Namibia still has no data protection statute, regulator or breach-notification duty**. The Data Protection Bill was last reported in January 2026 as awaiting resubmission to the Cabinet Committee on Legislation, and no later step is confirmed. For Namibian customers, MOTION's obligations therefore rest on contract, Article 13 of the Constitution and the Electronic Transactions Act, whose e-signature provisions took effect on 15 June 2026. South Africa's POPIA, by contrast, is fully in force. It makes MOTION an **operator** that must tell customers **"immediately"** of a suspected compromise. It also treats MOTION's **US-hosted service** as a section 72 cross-border transfer that needs a binding agreement. VAT is **15% in both countries**, and South Africa's planned 15.5% rate was reversed. Registration thresholds have moved: Namibia's is **N$1 million**, and South Africa's became **R2.3 million on 1 April 2026**. Namibia reset its procurement thresholds on **4 August 2026** (GN 259 of 2026). South African municipal quotation bands have been **R2,000 up to R200,000, R300,000 or R750,000** since December 2023. Every one of those thresholds includes VAT. Mandatory e-invoicing is coming in both countries, but not before about 2028. In the market, Workshop Software has priced in rand since 1 December 2025, at **R1,100 to R2,900 a month**. That puts MOTION's N$1,200 and N$2,400 tiers level with the benchmark rather than below it. Public bodies buy fleet maintenance as bundled services, and South Africa's national contract RT46-2026 gives state maintenance to Afrirent until June 2031. MOTION's website plan has already absorbed the market correction. Sixteen claims elsewhere are stale. The most consequential are a 72-hour breach window, a price-ceiling framing in the PRD and benchmark, the claim of "hosting nearer than the US", and a data inventory that predates requisitions and the floor app.

## Namibia's data protection bill stalls, so contract and the ETA govern

The bill's timeline is a series of missed targets. The ICT Ministry's 2025/26 budget motivation promised tabling in that financial year ([Parliament of Namibia, Vote 29](https://www.parliament.na/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Vote-29-Information.pdf)). In August 2025 the minister expected it in Parliament by September or October 2025 ([NAMPA](https://www.nampa.org/text/22709136)). After the NSFAF breach in October 2025, Nam-CSIRT called for the law to be finalised urgently. CRAN conceded that institutions lack breach-handling protocols because no comprehensive legal framework exists ([The Namibian](https://www.namibian.com.na/calls-for-urgent-finalisation-of-data-protection-law-after-nsfaf-data-breach/)). The latest dated primary report is from **27 January 2026**: Minister Theofelus said drafting was complete and the bill was "ready for resubmission" to the Cabinet Committee on Legislation, with no tabling date ([New Era](https://neweralive.na/data-legislations-under-final-polishing/)). DLA Piper's handbook, revised on 20 March 2026, lists **no authority, no registration, no transfer restrictions and no breach-notification duty** in Namibia ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NA)). Some aggregator claims say the bill was tabled in late 2025, and one investment-agency document refers to a "Data Protection Act (2022)". Both conflict with the minister's January statement and should be treated as errors ([New Era](https://neweralive.na/data-legislations-under-final-polishing/)). Chambers reports that promulgation is "expected" by the end of 2026, but no passage is confirmed ([Chambers Doing Business In 2026](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/Doing-Business-In-2026/718/)). Two items could change this picture and remain unverified: an unlocated 2026/27 budget document said to report Cabinet Committee approval, and a Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr alert of 7 October 2026 that could not be read ([CDH alert](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/news/publications/2026/Namibia/Corporate-Commercial/Corporate-and-Commercial-alert-7-October-2026-An-update-on-Namibias-digital-infrastructure-and-legal-framework)).

The current text of the bill is not public, but the published drafts show where it is heading. They provide for an independent **Data Protection Supervisory Authority** and reach controllers and processors wherever they are located. Consent is the primary basis for processing. Cross-border transfers would be permitted where the recipient's law or agreement gives similar protection, where the data subject consents, or where a contract requires the transfer ([ENSafrica, 2024](https://www.ensafrica.com/news/detail/9421)). Data-subject rights are a weak point: a section on them was removed in 2020 and, according to IPPR, has not been substantially restored ([Namibian Sun](https://www.namibiansun.com/local-news/data-protection-bill-returns-to-parliament2025-08-18167195)).

Until the bill passes, the Electronic Transactions Act 4 of 2019 is the statute that bites. Most of it has been in force since 16 March 2020. **Section 20 (electronic signatures) and Chapter 5 (voluntary security accreditation by CRAN) took effect on 15 June 2026** by GN 182/2026. Chapter 4, which covers online consumer protection and a 7-day cooling-off right, is still not in force ([LAC annotated ETA](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Electronic%20Transactions%20Act%204%20of%202019.pdf); [Government Gazette 8949](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/2026/8949.pdf)). Four provisions bear directly on MOTION, all in the [LAC annotated ETA](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoSTAT/Electronic%20Transactions%20Act%204%20of%202019.pdf):

- **Electronic signatures.** Under section 20(2), an ordinary electronic signature is valid where the parties agree to use it.
- **Record retention.** Section 24 lets electronic records satisfy a legal duty to keep records if four conditions are met: the record is a data message; it is kept in its original format or one shown to represent it accurately; its origin, destination and the dates and times it was generated, sent, received and first retained can be identified; and it meets any prescribed requirement. Under section 24(3), the workshop **remains liable** if its service provider fails to keep the records.
- **Service provider status.** Section 49 defines anyone who stores or backs up data as a "service provider", which includes MOTION.
- **Hosting safe harbour.** Section 52(2) protects a service provider from liability for third-party data only if it has **designated an agent for take-down notices** and published the agent's contact details.

MOTION's documents publish no such agent. One older rule also deserves attention given MOTION's US hosting. NamRA's VAT brochure says accounting records are to be kept "at the place of business in Namibia". The brochure dates from 2018 and still shows the old registration threshold ([NamRA VAT brochure](https://itas.namra.org.na/assets/documents/other-forms/Value_Added_Tax_Brochure.pdf)).

## POPIA makes MOTION an operator with an "immediately" clock

For the data its customers load, MOTION is an **operator**: under section 1, a person who processes for a responsible party "in terms of a contract or mandate". The workshop or municipality is the **responsible party** ([POPIA s1](https://popia.co.za/section-1-definitions/)). Section 20 confines MOTION to processing with the customer's knowledge or authorisation and requires confidentiality ([POPIA s20](https://popia.co.za/section-20-information-processed-by-operator-or-person-acting-under-authority/)). Section 21(1) requires the customer to have a **written contract** binding MOTION to the section 19 security measures. Section 21(2) requires MOTION to notify the customer **"immediately"** where there are reasonable grounds to believe personal information has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person ([POPIA s21](https://popia.co.za/section-21-security-measures-regarding-information-processed-by-operator/)). The customer then reports to the Information Regulator. Since **1 April 2025**, that report must go through the Regulator's **eServices portal** and no longer by email ([Information Regulator, 7 April 2025](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/MEDIA-STATEMENT-INVITATION-TO-REPORT-SECURITY-COMPROMISES-THROUGH-THE-eSERVICES-PORTAL-.pdf)).

Section 22 sets no hour count. Notice is due "as soon as reasonably possible" after discovery, and data subjects must be told in writing, with enough detail to protect themselves. A **72-hour deadline cited by vendor guides could not be found in the statute** ([POPIA s22](https://popia.co.za/section-22-notification-of-security-compromises/)). Enforcement is real and reaches municipalities. The Regulator reported **more than 8,000 compromise notifications** since commencement, more than 1,220 of them since 1 April 2026. It fined **Blouberg Local Municipality R500,000**, which a court reduced to R250,000 ([ITLawCo, September 2026](https://itlawco.com/information-regulator-briefing-2026/)).

Every responsible party must register an **Information Officer** before that person takes up duties. For a municipality it is the **Municipal Manager**; for a company it is the CEO, the MD or a manager they authorise. Registration details must be updated at least yearly. A multinational based outside South Africa must authorise an IO in South Africa and designate a South African-based deputy ([Regulator Guidance Note on Information Officers](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Guidance_Note_on_Information_Officers_.pdf)). The research notes infer from this that MOTION, a Namibian close corporation, would need a South African-based IO for any processing where it is itself the responsible party, such as its own billing contacts. Whether POPIA reaches that processing at all is a question for counsel. No new registration rule or guidance note was issued in 2025–2026 ([ITLawCo](https://itlawco.com/information-regulator-briefing-2026/)).

The amended POPIA Regulations of **17 April 2025** changed the rules MOTION's messaging features touch. **"Opt-out" does not constitute consent** for electronic direct marketing. Objection and correction requests must be free and accepted through any channel, WhatsApp and SMS included. The duty to keep a PAIA manual was removed from regulation 4 ([Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/south-africa-amendments-to-the-popia-regulations-key-changes-you-need-to-know/)). The Regulator treats telephone calls as electronic direct marketing, which industry disputes, and a court ruling is expected. Consumer Protection Act regulations gazetted on **15 April 2026** created a national opt-out registry, which does not replace POPIA consent ([ITLawCo](https://itlawco.com/information-regulator-briefing-2026/)). The practical line is this. Job-assignment and approval notifications to staff are not marketing. **Service-due reminders to vehicle owners probably are**, and need opt-in consent or the existing-customer exception, with an opt-out in every message.

On cross-border transfers, section 72 allows a transfer abroad on five grounds. For B2B SaaS the workable one is a law, binding corporate rules or a **binding agreement** that gives adequate protection, including limits on onward transfer ([POPIA s72](https://popia.co.za/section-72-transfers-of-personal-information-outside-republic/)). Commentators treat offshore hosting and offshore staff access as transfers. **No South African adequacy list exists** ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/zaf/legal-updates/Managing-cross-border-data-transfers)), and no source treats encryption as an exemption. An academic view holds that section 72 binds the exporting responsible party rather than the operator, but that article could not be retrieved ([Coetzee, PER 2024](https://www.saflii.org/za/journals/PER/2024/48.html)). Either way, the clause belongs in the operator agreement the customer signs. Special personal information or children's data sent to an inadequate jurisdiction needs the Regulator's **prior authorisation** under section 57(1)(d) ([Guidance Note](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Guidance_Note_on_Information_Officers_.pdf)).

For employee data, the Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA) section 31 requires employers to keep records of **time worked** and remuneration for **three years from the last entry**. A search summary reports that employers with fewer than five employees are excluded ([BCEA s31](https://source.acts.co.za/basic-conditions-of-employment-act-1997/bcoe97_31__keeping_of_records.php)). The staff data MOTION holds (names, pay numbers, clocked time, sign-off names and job assignments) is ordinary personal information. It would become special personal information only if MOTION added fingerprint or face clocking ([Meyer Attorneys](https://meyerattorneys.co.za/2025/11/14/biometric-timekeeping-law/)).

## Procurement thresholds reset, and every figure includes VAT

Namibia's thresholds changed on **4 August 2026**, when GN 259 of 2026 (GG 8989) replaced regulations 2 and 3 and both annexures of the 2017 Public Procurement Regulations ([LAC annotated Regulations](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoREG/Public%20Procurement%20Act%2015%20of%202015-Regulations%202017-047.pdf)). The Finance Ministry said the figures were adjusted for inflation and currency depreciation since 2017. **All regional councils moved from Category 2 to Category 1** ([The Brief, 7 August 2026](https://thebrief.com.na/2026/08/namibia-streamlines-public-procurement-what-the-new-regulations-mean-for-government-and-business/)). Regulation 3(3) states that thresholds **include VAT**.

| Namibia (GN 259/2026) | Goods | Works | Non-consultancy services |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small value procurement (s38) | up to **N$30,000** | up to N$30,000 | up to N$30,000 |
| Request for sealed quotations (s32) | up to **N$3m** | up to N$3m | up to N$3m |
| Restricted bidding (s31(1)(b)) | up to N$5m | up to N$5m | up to N$5m |
| Open international bidding (s30(a)) | above N$45m | above N$70m | not applicable |
| Entity runs its own bids up to: Category 1 (regional councils, Part I municipalities) | N$45m | N$60m | N$25m |
| Category 2 (Part II municipalities) | N$35m | N$55m | N$20m |
| Category 3 (towns and villages) | N$30m | N$40m | N$10m |

Above the entity's limit, the Central Procurement Board runs the bid ([LAC annotated Regulations](https://www.lac.org.na/laws/annoREG/Public%20Procurement%20Act%2015%20of%202015-Regulations%202017-047.pdf)). Two caveats apply. Annexure 2 was read from the PDF's text layout, so it should be checked against the gazette before MOTION displays it as authoritative. The minimum number of quotations for each method was not confirmed. Secondary sites quoting a N$250,000 quotation limit or a N$5 million board threshold are out of date ([TenderPipe](https://tenderpipe.com/tenders-namibia/)).

South African municipalities have worked to **Notice 4198, Government Gazette 49863, of 14 December 2023** since its publication. It abolished the "written or verbal quotations" band. **Formal written price quotations** now cover purchases over R2,000 up to **R300,000 (local), R200,000 (district) or R750,000 (metro)**, VAT included, and competitive bidding is required above those values. Splitting a requirement to avoid bidding is prohibited ([National Treasury gazette](https://mfma.treasury.gov.za/RegulationsandGazettes/Municipal%20SCM%20Amendment%20Regulations.pdf)). Regulation 17 still requires quotations **from at least three accredited providers**. Where fewer are obtained, the reasons must be recorded and approved by the CFO ([reg 17](https://source.acts.co.za/municipal-finance-management-act-2003/n868_17__formal_written_price_.php)). Requirements **over R30,000** must also be advertised for at least seven days on the municipal website and notice board ([reg 18](https://source.acts.co.za/municipal-finance-management-act-2003/n868_18__procedures_for_procur.php)). Municipalities may set lower thresholds in their own policies, and some 2025/26 policies still show the old bands ([Sedibeng SCM Policy](https://www.sedibeng.gov.za/a_keydocs/2025-2026%20MFMAs/Budget%20related%20policies%2032141/15.%20Supply%20Chain%20Management%20Policy%20and%20Procedures%20%20-%20(2025-26).pdf)). The Public Procurement Act 28 of 2024 was still "to be proclaimed" at the latest check, so the MFMA regime governs ([CivicLedger](https://www.civicledger.co.za/news/public-procurement-act-28-2024-commencement-status/)).

The two regimes point MOTION's requisitions in different directions. MOTION's purchase requisitions run from about N$50 to N$500,000, and its lines record value **excluding** VAT ([requisitions-plan.md](../docs/requisitions-plan.md)). Every statutory band in both countries is VAT-inclusive, so a value band must be judged on the grossed-up figure. In Namibia, small value procurement ends at N$30,000. Most of MOTION's range therefore falls into sealed quotations, which the plan's own anti-goal hands to the council's procurement unit. In South Africa, the formal written quotation band reaches R200,000 to R750,000, so it is a natural fit for MOTION's three-quote flow. That flow would need an "advertised" flag above R30,000, a split-purchase warning, and the monthly report to the CFO that regulation 18(d) requires. Because the thresholds changed on effective dates (4 August 2026 in Namibia, 14 December 2023 in South Africa), rules should be stored per tenant and dated.

## Conclusion

The hosting location, not any statute, is now MOTION's main compliance exposure. One fact, the Phoenix server, drives the section 72 clause that South African customers need, makes the "hosting nearer" claim false, sits awkwardly with NamRA's place-of-business rule, and will meet the adequacy-style transfer test in Namibia's draft bill once it passes. The installed edition answers residency. The hosted edition still owes paperwork: a POPIA operator agreement with an "immediately" notice clause and a named backup country, and a published ETA take-down agent. None of that needs code. It needs counsel, and it is cheaper than losing a municipal tender on a questionnaire, at a time when the Regulator is fining municipalities.

Commercially, the evidence narrows MOTION's position. With the benchmark at par and local job-card tools at R149 to R999, MOTION's N$1,200 entry tier sits above the N$350 to N$600 band that its own plan says the first private customers will pay. Price cannot carry the first sale. Scope must: requisitions with quotations, the fleet route, the offline floor app. The most concrete new private segment is the 7,960 RT46 merchant garages, which must keep quotation and authorisation trails for state work. For public buyers, the procurement facts suggest a design rule. Store every legal figure (VAT rate, quotation band, retention period) with an effective date and a VAT basis, the way MOTION already stores tax on the document snapshot, because the record shows these figures changing every year or two.
